Changing the order didn’t change anything here: both structs are 16 bytes, even though the fields only add up to 13. In the original, 3 bytes of padding go after the char so the int starts at a multiple of 4. In the rearranged one, those 3 bytes just move to the end so the struct’s size stays a multiple of 8. Rearranging can save space in other cases (for example, char, double, int would take 24 bytes), but this field order was already as compact as it gets.
